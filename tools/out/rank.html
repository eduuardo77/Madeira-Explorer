The rank medal — four directions

The first attempt was five flat pills changing hue, and it read as a control rather than as a trophy. What makes metal look like metal is not its colour, it is how it handles light — bronze has a broad soft highlight, silver a tight cool one, gold a broad saturated one, platinum almost a mirror. Every medal below has a face gradient, a rim lit from the opposite side (that opposition is what reads as a bevel), and a specular sweep whose tightness is a property of the metal.

⚠ No SVG filters anywhere. feGaussianBlur is the obvious way to fake depth and it is unreliable in react-native-svg on Android — so everything here is gradients and paths, and what you are judging is something the app can actually draw.
⚠ The number is engraved, not printed — the same glyphs three times, dark below, light above, body colour over both.
⚠ Sketches, not shipping code. Only the direction you choose becomes a pure module in app/.

A — Medallion — the challenge coin

Concentric, military, unmistakably an *achievement*. The most familiar shape and the least specific to this product.

B — Wax seal — the travel object

Borrows from **travel** rather than from games. This app is a passport and a seal is the same family of object as a stamp. ⚠ My recommendation, and the one nobody named.

C — Enamel pin — the CS2 shape

Metal outline, enamel inside, specular **only on the metal edge**. Uses the app’s own mark as the whole silhouette rather than putting it inside something else.

D — Trophy plate — the PlayStation register

A shield. The most explicitly *game*, and the closest to the reference the project lead actually named.

All four again, on a light ground

⚠ The map is the one surface whose brightness the user chooses (D-026), and this button floats on it. A medal that only works on the dark ground is a medal that half the users never see properly — which is exactly how the settings control vanished when the dark map arrived.
